# Discovery notes (M0)

Five short conversations to confirm or cut problems P1–P5 and assumptions A1–A4 in the [PRD](PRD.md). **Not run yet.** Record participants by code only (D1–D5).

## Who to talk to

People who buy clothes online at least once a month, ideally a mix of people who have and haven't tried a camera search. Classmates, friends, family; at least two who aren't close friends, since friends are kind about ideas.

## Guide (20 minutes)

1. What was the last piece of clothing you bought online? How did you find it? *(A4)*
2. Have you ever seen something someone else was wearing and wanted it? What did you do? *(P1, P2)*
3. Open your camera roll: how many screenshots or photos of clothes are in there from the last month? What happened to them? *(P2)*
4. Have you used Google Lens, a shop's camera search or Pinterest Lens for clothes? Tell me about the last time. What came back? *(P3)*
5. When you look at a page of results, what makes one look "like" the thing you wanted? What makes one look wrong? *(P5, A1, Q2)*
6. How far down a page of results do you usually look before you give up or change the search? *(A2)*
7. If the shop didn't have anything like it, would you rather be told that, or see the closest things anyway? *(A3)*
8. What would make you trust a photo search's results?

Ask for the last time it happened, not opinions about what they'd want. "Would you use…?" questions predict little.

Question 5 matters most. The relevance rule (D-06) assumes type and colour come first. If three or more people answer with pattern, fit or style before colour, the rule is reconsidered before M2 and the outcome logged in `decisions.md`.
